Tornadoes in the United States (2020–2024)

Tornadoes are by far most prevalent in the USA. The dataset used contains all recorded tornadoes from 2020 to 2024.

How to navigate the visualisation: Use the map's state picker to see data for one state. Use the map's checkbox to include non-fatal tornadoes in the data. If a state does not have any fatal tornadoes, it will have at least one non-fatal tornado, so it is recommended to turn on this filter for such states. Both map filters will adjust other charts accordingly. The black dots display the state capital name when filtered for that state. The line chart's year picker filters data for the selected year across all charts. You may hover over data points on any chart for further info.

Locations of Fatal Tornadoes

Tornadoes appear concentrated in the eastern half of the country. This is where the infamous Tornado Alley resides, comprising of states like Texas, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Missouri and Alabama. The flat geographical terrain and warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico create ideal conditions for tornado formation.

The deadliest tornado during this period was an EF4 tornado on December 10, 2021, resulting in 57 fatalities. It impacted Arkansas, Missouri, Tennessee and Kentucky (aftermath in Kentucky pictured). This was one of 71 tornadoes in a two-day outbreak, which resulted in over $3.9 billion in property damages. However, most tornadoes did not result in any fatalities, as indicated by the grey dots (when the checkbox is ticked).

Tornadoes per Month

April 2020 was the deadliest month for tornadoes since March 2012. May 2024 had the highest monthly tornadoes (457) within the 5 years. These months coincide with peak tornado season (late spring to early summer), when atmospheric conditions are most favorable.

Tornado Track Length vs Path Width

There is a weakly positive correlation between the length of a tornado's track and its path width. Longer track lengths generally indicate more powerful tornadoes, which tend to have wider paths too.

We can also see a positive trend of EF ratings increasing with tornado width + length, going from blue (EF1) in the bottom left to red (EF4) in the northeasterly direction.

The widest tornado (3960 yards) caused 7 deaths, as part of an Easter Outbreak of 141 tornadoes and $3 billion worth of damage. The longest tornado (168.53 miles) was, again, the quad-state EF4 tornado from December 2021.

Distribution of losses

This box plot shows a clear positive relationship between EF rating and losses, from the rising median values, interquartile ranges and min/max ranges. It makes sense given the definitions of each EF rating.

It is interesting that the costliest tornado was an EF3 ($1504M), while the costliest EF4 tornado only caused $200M in damages. These damages most likely location-dependent, causing more losses in more built-up areas.

The range for EF1 is noticeably much smaller than other EF ratings, which is understandable given that EF1 is the second weakest category, thus it is very unlikely to cause much in losses.
